01

Executive Summary

ESBC-AI (Easy Service for Bangladeshi Citizens) is a bilingual (Bangla and English) AI agent that helps citizens complete government service tasks from home, replacing the agent form-filling shop, the broker, the lawyer errand and the repeated office trips.

12days, 12 to 23 Oct 2026
4layers: Know, Prepare, Track and Pay, Memory
13government service areas in scope
2languages: Bangla and English

Know

Answers service questions in plain Bangla or English, explains hard official wording simply, and cites the official source for every answer.

Prepare

Guides the application, fills the form from answers and phone scans, checks for missing or mismatched data, and builds a ready-to-submit packet.

Track and Pay

Follows each application through its stages, sends updates, and lets the citizen pay the official fee online (bKash and bank; simulated in the prototype).

Memory

A private, opt-in vault: “Save to Memory” keeps documents and key details so they never have to be re-collected or re-typed.

Core engineering: RAG over official documents, a LangGraph agent with tools, scan-quality checks, OCR and structured extraction, and strict hallucination control: when the knowledge base cannot support an answer, the agent says “I’m not sure”. The prototype delivers citation-grounded Q&A across the core services and one complete guided flow, and demonstrates Track and Pay against a simulated government office. Live submission to real government systems is described honestly as roadmap.

What is being built: a mobile-first web application, which is an installable web app that runs in the phone browser with no app-store download, with a FastAPI backend and the LangGraph agent behind it. It is not a native Android or iOS app; a native app is a roadmap item. This proposal page is only a static website that describes the project, not the product itself.

Problem Statement

Official information is scattered across portals, PDFs and circulars. Forms use formal, hard-to-understand Bangla and English. As a result many citizens depend on agent form-filling shops (small computer shops run by brokers, usually connected by phone, and often recommended by word of mouth or by office staff) and on lawyers. Each step adds cost, delay and errors. The cases below come from my own experience, my family, and an informal survey of people around me.

Passport (survey case, Birampur to Dinajpur)

A relative paid an agent shop to fill the form. Forms filled by brokers often contain errors, so submission took at least two days instead of one. A mother’s-name error carried over from the NID required a lawyer, an affidavit (with the SSC certificate and the mother’s documents), two court days and a hearing before the passport office would proceed. Four separate trips of about 50 km, plus lawyer and agent fees, added roughly 10,000 BDT. Filling the form correctly at home would have avoided most of it.

NID correction and address update (own experience)

At first issue, the registration agent entered wrong information (a parent’s name was recorded incorrectly). After I moved from Dhaka back to my home district, I also needed an address update. I filed both the correction and the address update through an agent, online and in person, about 1.5 to 2 years ago. The name is still not corrected and the address update is still pending, so I continue to use a record with wrong and outdated data, and that error then spreads into other documents.

Tax return

A taxpayer since 2006 has to hire a lawyer every year because the return form is too complicated; the citizen pays the tax and also a lawyer fee annually. The form’s wording is hard to follow even in Bangla, and harder in English.

Death certificate (2022)

Citizens are directed to a nearby agent shop, which charges about 1,200 to 1,500 BDT to fill the online form. After printing and submitting at the municipality, errors are found, so the citizen returns to the agent and pays again for corrections. Money is paid at two places and 5 to 6 days are lost.

Family pension transfer

My father retired as the Principal of a government college. After his death, my sister and I travelled to the district where he had served, to his former college for signatures, to the pension office and to the bank to move the pension and account to my mother’s name. Unofficial payments were demanded, and payments stopped for about five months while it was unresolved.

Lost marriage certificate

After moving house, documents were lost; re-issue needed a lawyer and the whole procedure again, about 5,000 BDT extra.

Notarization

Repeated physical visits to notary offices and lawyers for certificates needed abroad or officially; every notarization means a fee paid in person.

A day at the passport office (reported by people around me)

  1. From about 05:00The queue starts outside

    People arrive as early as 5 AM so that they are early in the line. The office itself opens at 9.

  2. 09:00The office opens

    Hours of standing before anyone is let in.

  3. Around 12:00 or laterForm submission

    Even after waiting since dawn, some people are not reached and cannot submit the form that day.

  4. AfternoonA second queue

    Fingerprints, a face photo and an iris scan are taken in another line.

  5. ↻ If a mistake is foundA printing or typing mistake sends you back to the start

    Most of the time the mistake is a typing or printing error. The citizen has to pay again and go through the same hassle, submitting everything from the beginning. The process loops back.

Option 1 · Wait

A full day of leave, hours of standing, and sometimes a return trip.

Option 2 · Pay a broker

It can shorten the time, but it costs money at every step. For ordinary citizens this is a negative effect: those who cannot pay lose the most time, and paying to skip the line becomes normal.

What ESBC-AI changes

  • A correct, complete form prepared at home, so the counter visit is not wasted
  • Where the official online application is available, online submission replaces the form-submission queue
  • The biometric visit stays physical; the goal is a booked time slot instead of a first-come line (roadmap, with the official system)
  • Typing and printing mistakes are caught at home by field checks (including name spelling against the NID) and a final review, so a second payment and a restart become much less likely
  • Official fees shown up front, so there is less pressure to pay a broker to save time

04

Evidence and Related Work

Before proposing a solution, I checked official sources and public reporting on 9 October 2026 to test whether the problem is real and whether the proposal fits what already exists. Findings are paraphrased, and every detail is re-verified against the live official site when it is ingested into the knowledge base.

Passport: the portal exists, the friction is in the form

The e-passport portal already lets citizens apply online and book a biometric appointment. Appearing in person for fingerprints, iris scan and signature is mandatory, and the printed application with its barcode and the appointment summary must be brought. The birth certificate must also be verifiable online. The opportunity is not a new portal; it is helping people fill the existing form correctly and arrive with everything ready.

Death registration: online form, then paper

A death is to be registered within 45 days, free of charge, with a late fee after that. The online application is filled first, and the printed form with documents is then submitted to the local government office. Listed documents include the deceased person’s identity or birth record and proof of death from a health provider, a graveyard authority, a local government administrator or another document. This matches the two-step, agent-shop pattern described in the problem statement.

NID corrections depend on the type of change

Corrections are filed online on the NID portal and grouped into categories that depend on what changes, each with different supporting documents. Changing a parent’s name typically needs education certificates and attested NID copies of the parents, and an address change typically needs a house deed, utility bill or rent receipt. Public reporting has also described brokers being detained for taking large sums from NID applicants. This is the basis for the Correction Pathway Guide.

Social allowances: rules and windows keep changing

Applications for old-age, widow and disability allowances open in seasonal online windows. For 2026-27 the window was extended more than once, and disability applications were later kept open until further notice. Eligibility details differ between older district notices and newer policy documents, and the disability allowance requires a Subarna Nagorik Card. This is why the Benefit Finder reads the newest official notice with its date and flags conflicts.

Registration policy points the same way

A 2026 policy brief on civil registration in Bangladesh recommends simpler procedures and secure online platforms so that people are not required to travel repeatedly to complete registration. The goal of ESBC-AI is aligned with that direction.

Government digital services already exist

The government has launched Nagorik Sheba Bangladesh, a one-stop online platform with service centres run by trained local entrepreneurs; pilot centres opened in Dhaka in May 2025, with around 100 services planned at the start and more ministries being connected. The a2i programme also runs the myGov app and the ekSheba entrepreneur centres (390 or more services), and announced the Sathi virtual assistant in December 2023. ESBC-AI is an independent assistive layer that helps citizens use official services; it is not affiliated with any of them, it does not compete with them, and it could integrate with them in future.

Design decisions that follow from the evidence

  • Position the product as an assistive layer over official portals, not a replacement; the biometric visit stays physical.
  • Every source carries a date; when sources conflict, the newest official source wins and the conflict is flagged.
  • Correction types are classified by rules read from the official source, not typed in by hand.
  • The Benefit Finder shows whether an application window is open.
  • Deadlines such as the 45-day registration period are shown as reminders.

Sources reviewed (9 Oct 2026): UNESCAP CRVS country profile for Bangladesh; WHO South-East Asia CRVS profile 2025; Bangladesh National Portal (union parishad notices); Vital Strategies CRVS policy brief 2026; e-passport portal and Bangladesh mission guidance; UNB (NID correction and e-passport guides); Dhaka Tribune; BSS (DSS notices, July to September 2026); the social protection portal; the a2i portal. Details can change, so each one is re-verified at ingestion.

Motivation

My father retired as the Principal of a government college. When he passed away, my sister and I had to travel back to the district where he served: to his former college for signatures, to the pension office, to the bank and other government offices, while grieving, and our family income stopped for months. I do not want another family to go through that because they could not find the right information, could not understand the forms, or could not afford the right intermediary. As an AI/ML engineer, I can use retrieval, agents, OCR and good product design to reduce that suffering, and I want a capstone I can keep improving after the bootcamp.

Proposed Solution

Layer 1 · Know

  • Ask in Bangla or English (voice later)
  • Retrieval from a curated official knowledge base
  • Every answer shows source, link and date checked
  • Low confidence leads to “I’m not sure”
  • Shows the official fee and processing time
  • Plain-Language Mode: explains hard official words and form fields in everyday Bangla or English, with the original wording beside it
  • Benefit Finder: a few questions (age, income, disability, widowhood) list the allowance schemes a citizen may qualify for, with sources and whether the application window is open

Layer 2 · Prepare

  • Checklist loaded per service
  • Conversational form filling, one plain-language question per field
  • Phone scan with a guide and live quality check (blur, glare, cropping)
  • OCR and vision extraction with per-field confidence
  • Cross-document checks and a Correction Pathway Guide: if a record such as the NID has an error, it identifies the type of correction and which record to fix first, and lists the supporting documents usually needed (rules read from the official source)
  • One packet PDF; the citizen confirms every field

Layer 3 · Track and Pay

  • Stateful case tracker (LangGraph checkpoints)
  • Draft, Submitted, Under review, Approved, Ready
  • Email updates and appointment dates
  • Online fee payment (bKash and bank): sandbox and simulated in the prototype
  • Case Navigator for life events, e.g. death, pension, bank
  • Prototype uses a government office simulator

Layer 4 · Memory (My Vault)

  • Save to Memory: opt-in storage for scanned documents and key details (parents’ names, address, ID numbers)
  • Autofills future forms, so nothing is typed twice
  • Helps after loss: shows which documents you hold and where replacements come from
  • View, export or delete anything at any time; encrypted; synthetic data in the demo
  • Roadmap: expiry reminders (e.g. passport)

User Journey and Citizen Guide

The whole process, from home

  1. 1Get the form
  2. 2Fill it with guided chat
  3. 3Scan documents with the phone
  4. 4AI checks and fixes mistakes
  5. 5Pay the fee (bKash or bank)
  6. 6Submit and track
  7. 7Visit only for biometrics or pickup

How to scan documents with your phone (built-in guide)

Place the paper flat on a plain table in good light.

Keep all four corners inside the frame.

Avoid shadows and glare. The app warns you if the photo is blurry.

Check the preview, retake if needed, then confirm.

Example: Death Certificate

  1. 1Opens the app on a phone, chooses Bangla
  2. 2Asks what is needed for a death certificate; gets the checklist, the 45-day rule, the official fee and sources
  3. 3Answers one plain-language question at a time about the deceased and the applicant (saved details are autofilled from Memory)
  4. 4Scans the NID and doctor’s certificate with the phone guide; fields are extracted and shown
  5. 5Agent flags a mismatch (e.g. a parent’s name spelling) and explains the correction pathway before submission
  6. 6Reviews and confirms; the application packet PDF is generated
  7. 7Pays the official fee (simulated bKash or bank), submits; the simulator moves Received, Under review, Ready; email updates follow
  8. 8Case Navigator suggests next steps (pension, bank) with checklists; the citizen taps Save to Memory

One phone session: no agent shop, no broker, no repeated correction trips.

System Architecture

Ingestion (offline)

Official documents and FAQs, then extraction and cleaning, chunking with metadata (service, URL, date checked, language), embeddings, and the vector store.

Agent loop (online)

Intent router (question, apply, upload, status, memory). Questions: hybrid retrieval, re-rank, plain-language rewrite, grounded answer with citations, or polite refusal. Applications: form subgraph, scan check, extraction, validation, confirmation, packet, payment, case, optional Save to Memory.

11

AI/ML Components

RAG with citations

Chunks carry source, page, URL and date; prompts forbid unsupported claims.

Hallucination control

Re-ranker score plus groundedness check; below threshold, the agent says “I’m not sure”.

Plain-language rewriting

Rewrites formal Bangla and English into everyday wording, always shown next to the original and the source, so meaning is not lost.

Document intelligence

Scan guide with live quality checks (blur, glare, cropping, retake prompts), then OCR and a vision model with per-field confidence; low-confidence fields are highlighted for the citizen to correct.

Validation and correction pathway

Rule-based and LLM-assisted name, date and relationship checks; when a record has an error, the agent explains which record to correct first and the usual supporting documents.

Memory

Opt-in profile facts and documents saved with “Save to Memory”, used to autofill and to recall what the citizen already holds.

Benefit Finder

Eligibility questions mapped to scheme rules read from the official source, with the date checked and the application window status; amounts are never hard-coded, and when sources conflict the newest official one wins and the conflict is flagged.

Agent tools

search_service_info, simplify_text, get_checklist, fill_form_field, check_scan_quality, extract_from_scan, validate_documents, generate_packet, create_payment, create_case, get_case_status, save_to_memory, recall_memory.

Bilingual handling and privacy

Language detection, same-language answers, Bangla and English glossary; PII masking in logs, consent for Memory, deletion on request.

Ethics, Privacy and Legal

  • Clearly stated on screen as a bootcamp capstone project (AI & ML Engineering Bootcamp, Module-27, Batch-6, Ostad), not an official government service and not affiliated with any government office.
  • Consent and data minimization: only fields needed for the chosen service.
  • Case scans are deleted after closure unless the citizen chooses Save to Memory. Memory is opt-in, encrypted, and can be viewed, exported or deleted at any time.
  • NID and family data are masked in logs and encrypted at rest.
  • No legal advice; answers describe official procedures. Whether a digitally returned certified or notarized copy is accepted depends on the rules, so it is a roadmap item to verify with the proper authorities.
  • Before any real pilot: review Bangladeshi data protection and digital security requirements and seek approvals.
  • Mobile-first, simple language; voice option on the roadmap for lower literacy.

PROJECT PROPOSAL

ESBC-AI

Easy Service for Bangladeshi Citizens

Farjana Ferdausi · AI & ML Engineering Bootcamp, Module-27, Batch-6, Ostad · October 2026

1. Problem statement

Government services in Bangladesh are essential, yet completing them is slow, costly and error-prone. Official information is scattered across portals, PDFs and circulars in formal Bangla and English, and the forms are hard to fill correctly. Many citizens therefore depend on agent shops, brokers and lawyers, and one typing or printing mistake can force them to pay again and restart the whole process. Official online portals already exist (for example for passports, birth and death registration and NID), so the remaining friction is not access but correct completion: understanding the form, matching documents and fixing errors before submission.

Evidence collected from my own experience, my family and an informal survey, cross-checked with public sources:

  • Passport correction: a mother’s-name error carried over from the NID led to a lawyer, an affidavit, two court days and four trips of about 50 km, costing about 10,000 BDT extra.
  • Death certificate (2022): agent-shop fees of about 1,200 to 1,500 BDT, corrections paid twice and 5 to 6 days lost. The official process is an online form, then a printed copy submitted at a local government office.
  • NID correction and address update: still pending after 1.5 to 2 years, and the wrong record then spreads into other documents.
  • Tax return: a lawyer is hired every year because the form wording is too hard to follow.
  • Passport office day: queues from about 5 AM, form submission around noon or later, a second queue for biometrics, and brokers selling speed.
2. Objectives and success criteria

The goal is a bilingual assistive agent that reduces errors, intermediary costs and physical visits. Targets for the prototype, measured on a labelled evaluation set:

Answers

Top-5 retrieval hit rate 85% or higher; faithfulness (RAGAS) 0.85 or higher; correct source on 90% of answers; correct “I’m not sure” on 90% of out-of-scope questions.

Documents

Field extraction accuracy 85% or higher on synthetic documents; injected mismatches and typing errors detected in 80% or more of test cases.

Experience

Clarity rating of 4 out of 5 from five test users; physical visits per application cut from 4 (the passport survey case) to 1, for biometrics only.

3. Proposed solution

ESBC-AI (Easy Service for Bangladeshi Citizens) is a mobile-first, bilingual (Bangla and English) web application that acts as an assistive layer over official services, not a replacement. It has four layers:

Know

Plain-language answers in Bangla or English with the official source and date for every claim, official fees and deadlines (such as the 45-day registration period), and a Benefit Finder for social allowances.

Prepare

Guided form filling, phone document scanning with a quality check, cross-document validation, a Correction Pathway Guide and a ready-to-submit application packet.

Track and Pay

A stateful case tracker with email updates and simulated bKash and bank payment, run against a government-office simulator in the prototype.

Memory

An opt-in, encrypted vault: “Save to Memory” stores documents and key details, autofills future forms and helps after documents are lost.

Prototype scope: citation-grounded Q&A across 13 service areas (birth and death registration, passport, NID, tax, social allowances, land mutation, driving licence, police clearance and more) and one complete guided flow, the Death Certificate. It is not an official government service and is not affiliated with any government office.

4. Related work and positioning

The government already runs Nagorik Sheba Bangladesh, myGov and the ekSheba centres, announced the Sathi virtual assistant, and official portals offer online forms and appointments. ESBC-AI complements them with capabilities that public descriptions of those services do not highlight: the source and date on every claim, a refusal when unsure, plain-language explanation, cross-document checks with a correction pathway, and scan-to-packet preparation. Design decisions that follow from the evidence: every source is dated; when sources conflict the newest official one wins and the conflict is flagged; the biometric visit stays physical.

5. System architecture

Ingestion (offline): official documents and FAQs, text extraction and cleaning, chunking with metadata (service, URL, date checked, language), embeddings, vector store. Agent loop (online): an intent router sends questions to hybrid retrieval, re-ranking, a plain-language rewrite and a grounded answer with citations (or a refusal), and applications to a form subgraph with scan check, extraction, validation, confirmation, packet, payment and case creation.
